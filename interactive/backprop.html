Forward & backward, step by step

This is Figure 1.3 from Chapter 1, made walkable. The model is f = w·x + b with input x = 2 and target y = 7; the loss is L = (f − y)². Press Step and watch the forward pass compute and record each value left to right — then keep pressing and watch the backward pass carry the sensitivity ∂L/∂· right to left, multiplying in one local derivative per node. When both gradients arrive, take a gradient step and see the loss fall. Then move the sliders and run it again from anywhere.

Two facts to notice while you play. The backward pass visits each node about as often as the forward did — that is why the full gradient costs roughly two extra forward passes, for two parameters or seven billion. And the forward values (blue) must be kept until backward consumes them: at scale, those stored activations — not the weights — are what fill a training GPU (Chapters 1.3 and 15).
