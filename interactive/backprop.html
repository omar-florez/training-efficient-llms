Backpropagation, step by step

A two-layer network with the standard symbols: input x, weights W₁, W₂, logits z = W·input, sigmoid activations h = σ(z), prediction ŷ, and an MSE loss L = ½(ŷ − y)². Press Step: the forward pass computes and records each value left to right; the backward pass then walks right to left, and at every weight layer it pays two gradients — ∂L/∂W for the update and ∂L/∂x (the input side) to keep the chain moving. When both ∂L/∂W arrive, take an SGD step W ← W − α·∂L/∂W and watch the loss fall.

Press Step to begin the forward pass.

Two facts to notice while you play. Every backward line reuses a stored forward value (h₁, ŷ, x…) — that is why activations must be kept until backward consumes them, and why at scale they, not the weights, fill a training GPU. And each weight layer pays two gradients of equal size — ∂L/∂W and ∂L/∂x — which is exactly why the backward pass costs about twice the forward (Chapter 8).
