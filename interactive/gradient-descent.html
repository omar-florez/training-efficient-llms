This is the rent model from Chapter 1 — three apartments, loss L(w) = mean squared error, intercept fixed at zero so we can draw the bowl. You control the one hyperparameter that matters: the learning rate. Start with the default and press Run: the ball rolls to the bottom. Then drag the slider to the right and run again — watch the steps overshoot, bounce higher each time, and diverge. You are reproducing the most common training failure in deep learning, safely.

Vertical axis is log-scaled loss, so both convergence and explosion stay on screen. The dashed line is the noise floor: the best any straight line can do on this data. Divergence threshold for this problem: η ≈ 1.3×10⁻⁴.

Why it diverges: the update is w ← w − η·dL/dw, and on a quadratic bowl the step size that keeps you descending is bounded by the curvature — beyond η = 2/(2·mean(x²)) each step lands farther from the minimum than the last. Chapter 1, Section 1.6 tells this story; Chapter 17 manages this knob over a three-month run.
