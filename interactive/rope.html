RoPE: positions as rotations

RoPE tells the model where a token is by rotating its query and key vectors — position 17 means "rotate by 17 × θ". The remarkable consequence: the attention score between a query at position m and a key at position n depends only on the distance m − n, never on where the pair sits. See it yourself: press Slide both and watch the two vectors spin together like a rigid body while the score does not move. Then drag just one slider and watch the score change — because now the distance changed.

The frequency ladder: every dimension pair is a clock hand

A head does not use one rotation speed — dimension pair i rotates at θᵢ = base−2i/d radians per position. Fast pairs distinguish neighboring tokens; slow pairs distinguish distant regions, like the hands of a clock. Drag position and watch; then switch the base from Llama 2's 10,000 to Llama 3's 500,000 and notice the slow hands slow down further — that is literally the whole context-extension trick of Chapter 21: stretch the slow clocks so positions stay distinguishable over a longer horizon.

Eight of a head's 64 dimension pairs (dhead = 128). The leftmost hand turns a full radian per token; the rightmost barely moves across the whole context. Together they give every position a unique multi-speed reading, and every offset a unique pattern of angle differences.

Why this design won: the rotation adds no parameters, applies in every layer (position information never has to survive the residual stream), and makes attention natively relative — "the token 3 to my left" is the same geometric relationship at position 10 or position 10,000. Chapter 3.4 introduces it; Chapter 12 weighs its variants; Chapter 13 shows where it collides with latent attention; Chapter 21 stretches it.
